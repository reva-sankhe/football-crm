That exact 1-week ÷ 4-week-average approach has been widely used in team-sport research.

But I would change several things before treating this as production-ready.

1. Fix the date windows first

This one is a genuine calculation bug if you're currently getting 8 and 29 dates.

For an anchor date of 24 Aug, it should be:

Acute: 18–24 Aug = exactly 7 calendar days
Chronic: 28 Jul–24 Aug = exactly 28 calendar days

So in code, conceptually:

acute: anchor - 6 days through anchor
chronic: anchor - 27 days through anchor

Rest days count as zero load. You shouldn't switch to "last 3 sessions" just because you train three times weekly.

With three sessions per week, a normal seven-day window will usually contain roughly three sessions. Occasionally it may contain two or four because the schedule shifts — and that's actually useful information because workload has genuinely become compressed or spread out.

2. Require enough history

This is probably the biggest issue in your current implementation.

I would not calculate a normal ACWR label until the player has 28 complete calendar days of workload records.

Currently:

"not enough data" = chronic load is zero

means someone with only 10 days of history can receive a seemingly meaningful ACWR. That's misleading.

I'd make it:

<28 days of tracked history → "Building baseline"

And show:

18 days recorded. At least 28 days are required to establish a workload baseline.

Importantly, "history" should mean you're confident those days are actually covered by your data collection. A missing session shouldn't silently become a rest day.

3. I'd seriously consider uncoupling the baseline

This is the part of your calculation I'd question most.

Your current chronic value contains the acute week itself.

Imagine:

Previous weeks:

Week 1: 600
Week 2: 600
Week 3: 600
Current week: 1,200

Your calculation gives:

Chronic = (600 + 600 + 600 + 1200) / 4 = 750

ACWR = 1200 / 750 = 1.60

But what was the player actually accustomed to immediately before this week?

600/week.

So the current week is actually 2× their preceding baseline, but putting the spike into its own denominator pulls the ratio down.

That's the mathematical coupling issue raised in the ACWR literature.

A commonly used uncoupled version is:

Acute
= last 7 days

Baseline
= weekly average of the previous 21 days, excluding those acute 7 days

So:

Ratio=
previous 21 days/3
last 7 days
	​


This method has also been used in the literature specifically to avoid having the current acute week contribute to its own baseline.

For your app, I'd prefer this.

It has a much cleaner explanation to a coach:

How much work has this player done this week compared with what they averaged over the previous three weeks?

That's very easy to understand.

4. Because you train 3×/week, daily sRPE is still appropriate

Your underlying session-load method seems reasonable:

session load=duration in minutes×RPE

Then sum everything performed that calendar day.

So if Tuesday is:

60-minute football × RPE 6 = 360

Thursday:

75 × 7 = 525

Saturday:

60 × 5 = 300

Weekly acute load = 1,185 AU

That's the usual session-RPE approach used for internal training load. Session-RPE is also one of the most common workload variables used in ACWR studies.

And yes, combining multiple sessions on the same day is sensible.

5. Change your match calculation

This is where I see a bigger practical problem than the ACWR formula.

Currently:

match load = minutes played × RPE 7

So:

90 minutes = 630 AU

every time.

A brutal 90-minute game and an easy 90-minute game become identical.

If you're collecting player RPE anyway, I'd use:

Match RPE available → minutes played × player's match RPE

No RPE available → minutes × 7 as an estimated fallback

Then tag it internally as estimated.

For example:

Match	Minutes	RPE	Load
Match A	90	5	450
Match B	90	8	720

Your present system makes both 630.

During tournaments, that difference can substantially alter the 7-day load.

6. Your duplicate-prevention logic sounds correct

This part I would keep:

Match-grid minutes take priority; fallback match rows/orphans only added when not already covered.

And:

Multiple activities on the same date are summed.

Both are conceptually right.

I'd add automated tests for:

match recorded in grid + session table
two matches same day
match + training same day
substitute with 20 minutes
player attends but plays 0 minutes
missing RPE
orphan match record

Because duplicate match loading will wreck this metric much faster than small differences in ACWR methodology.

7. Don't estimate full-match load merely from attendance

This part I'd change:

Attendance on an orphan match day is estimated as full match duration × RPE 7.

That's quite a strong assumption.

A player could:

play 90 minutes
play 10 minutes
be an unused substitute

and you'd assign them the same workload.

I'd prefer:

Known minutes → use them

No minutes + confirmed player participation → estimated load, clearly flagged

Attendance only → don't assume 90 minutes

Unless your app specifically defines "attendance" as "played full match".

8. Change the labels

I'd remove the medical-sounding labels completely.

For the ratio itself I'd use:

Ratio	Display
<0.8	Below Baseline
0.8–1.3	Typical Range
1.3–1.5	Elevated
1.5–2.0	High Spike
>2.0	Very High Spike

The 0.8–1.3 range appears repeatedly in the ACWR literature, including systematic reviews, but thresholds vary considerably between studies and should not be treated as universal injury cut-offs.

And I would not write:

Injury risk elevated. Consider reducing load.

The causal/predictive validity of ACWR for injury has been heavily challenged.

For your 2.16 screenshot:

Very High Workload Spike

Last 7-day load is 2.16× the player's recent weekly baseline. Review recent workload, recovery and upcoming sessions.

That's defensible.

Good. Keep those.

So yes: 7 days makes sense even though you only train three times per week. I wouldn't change that part. I'd change the baseline handling, data sufficiency, match loading, date boundaries and UI interpretation.
